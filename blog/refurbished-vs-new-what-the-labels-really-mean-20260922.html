Refurbished vs. New: What the Labels Really Mean

2026-09-22

Why refurbished items show such big discounts

Many of the largest discounts online are on refurbished or open-box products. That can be a genuinely good way to save, but only if you understand what you are buying. The label on the listing matters as much as the price.

The common labels

  • New: Unused, in original packaging, with the full manufacturer warranty.
  • Open box: Usually unused or barely used, but the packaging was opened, often from a return. Check whether all accessories are included.
  • Manufacturer or certified refurbished: Inspected and repaired by the maker or an approved partner. These usually come with a warranty, though often shorter than new.
  • Seller refurbished: Restored by the seller, not the manufacturer. Quality varies widely, so the seller's reputation and return policy matter more.
  • Used / pre-owned: Sold as-is or lightly tested. Read the condition description and photos carefully.

Questions to ask before buying refurbished

  • Who did the refurbishing: the manufacturer, a certified partner, or the seller?
  • How long is the warranty, and who honors it?
  • What is the return window, and who pays return shipping?
  • Are the battery, charger, and accessories included and original?
  • Is the discount measured against the new price or a realistic refurbished price?

Compare against the right price

A refurbished item listed as "60% off" is often being compared with the original new price. A fairer test is to compare it with the current new price and with other refurbished listings of the same model. If a new unit is on sale for only a little more, the new one may be the better value because of its full warranty.
